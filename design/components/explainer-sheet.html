Bench Press · set 2

Estimated 1RM

What it is

An estimate of the heaviest weight you could lift for a single rep, worked out from a set you actually did. You never have to test a true max to track it.

How it's worked out

weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

Your set

100 kg × 5
→ 100 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) = 117 kg

Why it's useful

It's the only fair way to compare sets that look nothing alike. Was 100 kg × 5 better than 90 kg × 8? Estimated 1RM says yes — 117 against 114. That comparison is what the trend line tracks.

Where it breaks down

  • Reliable to about 10–12 reps. Past that you're limited by endurance rather than strength and the formula returns fantasy numbers, so Gymmy shows nothing above 12 reps.
  • It never counts as a personal record. Records are only for weight you genuinely lifted.

RPE — how hard the set was

What it is

A 1–10 rating of effort, where 10 means you could not have done another rep.

How to use it — count the reps you had left

10 — nothing left  ·  9 — 1 more rep
8 — 2 more  ·  7 — 3 more
6 or less — easy, more than 3 left

Where it breaks down

  • It's a judgement call, and most people underrate how close to failure they were. It gets more accurate with practice.
  • Entirely optional — leave it blank and nothing breaks.

Warm-up exclusion — otherwise it looks like a bug

Why your totals look lower

Warm-up sets

Sets you mark as warm-ups are kept in your history but left out of volume totals, personal records and estimated 1RM.

Otherwise ramping up with the empty bar would drag your volume chart down and clutter your records with weights you weren't trying hard on. They stay visible in the session so you can see how you worked up to your top set.
